You didn't build a bad app. You built three good apps and hid the best one.
Can it become a category leader?

Yes — but only of a category it defines and names. Tempo will not out-log Strong or out-science RP. It can own "the calendar-native training planner for busy people who still want to train seriously." That's a real, winnable, un-owned category. Leadership requires narrowing to it, not broadening away from it.

$1M / $10M ARR

$1M is very achievable — ~2,000 annual subs at a corrected price. The engine and craft already justify it; you need the wedge visible, the paywall fixed, and one working acquisition channel. $10M requires proven retention, a compounding growth loop, and the Coach tentpole — a focused 2–3 year path, not a fantasy.

$100M ARR — the honest ceiling

Unlikely on the current shape. $100M fitness apps are either platform-scale content (Apple/Peloton), hardware-attached (WHOOP), or category-defining with a distribution machine. Tempo could get there only by becoming the default for a massive segment (busy professionals) with a growth engine it doesn't have yet. Don't optimize for this — earn $10M first, and the path either appears or it doesn't.

What's actually preventing success

Not code. Not design. Not the idea. It's three self-inflicted things: (1) the wedge is buried and unreliable, (2) the scope is diluting the message and the effort, and (3) there is zero evidence anyone retains, so every bet is blind. All three are fixable in a quarter of disciplined subtraction and hardening — the hardest kind of work for a builder this capable, because it means stopping.
